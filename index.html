Business Lines

Four subsidiaries.
One cyclical economy.

VLR Holdings operates through four business lines designed to support one another — creating a cyclical economy where each business generates value for the others.

01
Agriculture
Going-concern farms · Heritage farming · Roots Land Management

Going-concern farms & heritage farming

We cannot live without land, and looking after our land is our responsibility. We all need to eat. We all want to be healthy. We want to know where our food comes from and feel genuinely connected to it. VLR knows there is a better way. Sustainable agriculture that supports the communities around it, that shows a different path forward. Yes, it may mean less volume than industrial production, but it means respect: for the land, for nature, for the people living within it. We benefit, and we teach a new approach at the same time.

VLR's agriculture subsidiary focuses exclusively on viable, going-concern agricultural operations — farms with demonstrated productivity and a credible path to sustainable returns. The portfolio spans multiple farming sectors, selected for resilience, scalability, and alignment with responsible land stewardship.

Roots Land Management

The agriculture subsidiary operates under the positioning of Roots Land Management — a deliberate framing that speaks to international investors without regulatory friction. The brand covers regenerative agriculture, permaculture, and integrated water and energy management. It reflects how VLR approaches the land: with care, with long-term thinking, and with an awareness of the communities it touches.

02
Agritourism
Hospitality that serves the land and the people on it

Hospitality serving the land

Agriculture on its own, unless operating at industrial scale, can be economically challenging. There are peaks when the farm is at full steam, and there are quieter periods where little needs to be done. Not every harvest is equal, and the result is uneven revenue from one year to the next. But farms are beautiful. And beauty, thoughtfully offered, has value. VLR complements its farming operations with sustainable tourism where guests can enjoy the environment and even participate in the agricultural process itself. These guests become the best consumers of the produce and the most credible marketers of the experience.

VLR's agritourism offering uses agricultural downtime productively — activating land and staff during off-season periods through curated hospitality experiences. This is not tourism bolted onto farming. It is an integrated model where the land drives the experience and the guests drive the market for agricultural produce.

The cyclical economy logic

Agritourism guests become the clients and natural marketers of VLR's agricultural products. This closes the loop: the farm feeds the hospitality offering, and the hospitality guests feed the farm's commercial pipeline. It is an intentional design — not tourism bolted onto farming, but an integrated model where the land drives the experience and the guests drive the market for agricultural produce.

03
Technology
Engineering practice built for farming realities

An engineering practice built for farming realities

Farming is capital intensive. The machinery involved requires diligent care, specialist knowledge, and consistent attention — and when things go wrong, the cost is significant. VLR is actively pursuing multiple developments in the technology space precisely because investing in a top-tier engineering practice addresses these challenges at the root. The ambition goes beyond internal needs. VLR's engineering capability is being developed to serve its own operations first — and then to meet the demand that exists well beyond its own portfolio. Products and services that solve real farming problems for others, not just for VLR.

VLR's technology subsidiary is an engineering practice designed to address the specific operational and infrastructure needs of agricultural businesses. It exists to solve real problems that farms and rural hospitality operations face, and to develop products that serve both internal requirements and external market demand.

04
Movable Assets
Furniture and art as a revenue centre, not a cost line

Art and premium FF&E

It is troubling how quickly everything is replaced today, how little of what surrounds us is made to last, or designed to mean something. VLR wants to change that. By investing in furniture and fittings that genuinely increase in value, the portfolio offers something different: assets that appreciate, that move between properties, and that bring variety and character to every hospitality space they occupy.

VLR invests in appreciating movable assets — primarily art and premium FF&E (Furniture, Fixtures & Equipment). These assets serve a dual purpose: they are deployed across VLR's hospitality properties to differentiate the guest experience, while simultaneously building a portfolio of appreciating value.

The strategic rationale

Conventional hospitality treats FF&E as a cost centre — a depreciation line that erodes returns. VLR's model inverts this: by acquiring pieces with genuine appreciating value, the portfolio generates design variance across properties while building a separately recoverable asset base. FF&E becomes a revenue centre.
